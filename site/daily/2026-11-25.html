RoboDojo-Mirror: How a Simple Mirror Breaks Robot Policies

PaperManipulationEmbodied AI Fails the Mirror Test: How a Simple Mirror Breaks Robot Policies

What it is: A paper by Xuanchen Liu, Hejun Zhang, Boxuan Dong, Ying Wu, Heshuai Li, Wen Gao, Yonghong Tian, and Weihao Yu introduces RoboDojo-Mirror, a benchmark built on the RoboDojo simulation framework. It tests whether robot manipulation policies can handle scenes containing mirrors, which create reflections of objects and the robot itself. The benchmark compares a mirror-free baseline against single-mirror, dual-mirror, and mirrored-tabletop conditions while keeping task objectives, success criteria, and policy checkpoints unchanged.

Why it matters: Across five policies—including Vision-Language-Action models, World Action Models, and agentic policies—every mirror condition reduced aggregate success rates. Averaged over three mirror conditions, the drop ranged from 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points, reaching 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop. Because a mirror is also a physical obstacle, the authors compared it with geometrically matched non-reflective panels. They found that 50–88% of mirror-specific failures occurred without recorded robot–panel contact, so collisions alone do not explain the degradation. Trajectory and contact analyses suggest behaviors consistent with phantom targeting of reflected objects and spatial mislocalization of real ones. Real-robot experiments with π₀.₅ and FastWAM also showed lower success rates under mirror conditions.

Can you try it without a robot? Partially. The code is available on GitHub (iPPLab/robodojo-mirror), and you can run the RoboDojo simulation framework on a Mac to test pretrained policies with mirror scenes. However, full evaluation requires an NVIDIA GPU, so a Mac without one may only support limited experiments or demos.

Try it without a robot: Run the RoboDojo sim on Mac with mirrored scenes to probe pretrained policies; full eval needs an NVIDIA GPU.
