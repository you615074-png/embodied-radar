LocoMuJoCo: An Open Benchmark for Whole-Body Motion Imitation

PaperRLLocoMuJoCo: A Comprehensive Imitation Learning Benchmark for Whole-Body Control

What it is. LocoMuJoCo is an open-source benchmark from Firas Al-Hafez, Davide Tateo, Oleg Kaidanov, Aditya Bhatt, Artur Zelik, Zhengyi Luo, and Jan Peters for imitation learning and reinforcement learning on whole-body control. Per the paper, it puts MuJoCo (CPU) and the MJX / MjWarp GPU backends under one API, and ships 9 humanoid, 3 (musculo-)skeletal, and 4 quadruped embodiments. Each humanoid comes with a curated native motion-capture dataset, and the library can also retarget AMASS and LAFAN1 motions onto its robots.

Why it matters. Whole-body imitation work is scattered across papers, each with its own robot model, motion set, and training code, which makes results hard to compare. LocoMuJoCo tries to fix that with a modular plug-in design: observations, rewards, control modes, terrains, and domain randomization are interchangeable, and compact JAX implementations of several RL and IL algorithms give JIT-compiled end-to-end training. A companion repo, loco-mujoco-real, covers sim-to-real deployment. It is MIT-licensed.

Can you try it without a robot? Yes, partly. MuJoCo's CPU backend runs on a Mac, so you can run the bundled examples and train a small policy on a quadruped or humanoid in simulation. The GPU-accelerated MJX / MjWarp path expects an NVIDIA setup and is best skipped on an Apple Silicon machine.

Try it without a robot: On Mac, install MuJoCo CPU backend and run the repo's example scripts to make a quadruped or humanoid imitate mocap data; skip the GPU backend.
