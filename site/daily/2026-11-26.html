Embo-Harness: Using Frontier Multimodal Models Directly as Robot Policies

PaperVLA & foundation modelsEmbo-Harness: Towards Understanding Frontier-Model-as-Policy Harness

What it is. Embo-Harness is a framework from Xiao-Ming Wu, Kang Liao, Yuexiao Ma and colleagues for studying what happens when you use a frontier multimodal model directly as a robot policy — no separate trained policy network, just a big model in a closed loop with observations in and actions out. The paper breaks the loop into four parts (prompting, planning, action execution, tool use) and distills six empirical findings into a practical recipe.

Why it matters. Most robot learning assumes you train a policy. This line of work asks whether a general model plus the right interface (the "harness") is enough. The recipe favors interfaces that use the model's native visual reasoning and tool use over handcrafted plans, skills, or action priors, and gives concrete choices for action representation, action chunking, reasoning effort, visual observations, short multi-action sequences, and policy refinement. On the RoboLab-120 benchmark, Embo-Harness reports 83.3% success, 26.2 points above the strongest reported baseline, and 15/20 zero-shot success across four real manipulation tasks. Per the paper, controlled interventions suggest the model relies more on accurate spatial target estimation than on explicit low-level joint or trajectory generation.

Can you try it without a robot? Not fully. The project page has demos, but the code and weights are not clearly public; reproducing the prompt harness would need a cloud GPU, and a local closed loop on a Mac is not realistic. Two caveats: the real-world validation is only 20 trials, and I could not independently verify the arXiv listing, so treat the numbers as the paper's own claims.
