T-Rex: A Bimanual Tactile Dataset in LeRobot Format

DatasetDatasetszekaiwang/trex_dataset

What it is. T-Rex is a large-scale teleoperation dataset from the T-Rex project (Zhuoyang Liu et al.; paper on arXiv). It was collected on a Dexmate Vega-1 dual-arm robot fitted with two Sharpa Wave dexterous hands. The data covers 5,464 episodes, about 50 hours at 30 fps, 22 motor primitives and 207 objects, with 5,370 language-annotated trajectories. Each frame has three RGB views (head plus two wrists), 10 raw tactile images and 10 deformation maps per hand, 6-axis fingertip wrenches, and 58-dim joint states and targets. It ships as a LeRobotDataset v3.0.

Why it matters. Most open manipulation datasets have parallel-jaw grippers and no touch. T-Rex pairs dexterous multi-finger hands with fingertip tactile and force readings, which is exactly the sensing that grasping and in-hand manipulation research needs. The hardware and teleoperation stack are also open-sourced, and there is a Colab notebook for loading the data.

Can you try it without a robot? Partly. You can load the dataset with LeRobot on any machine and replay videos and tactile signals in the notebook. Training a policy that transfers is not realistic here: the action space is 58-dimensional, the hands are special hardware, and the dataset is new, so community baselines are not yet established. Per the dataset card, watch first, train later.

Try it without a robot: Open the official Colab notebook to load the LeRobot dataset and explore videos plus tactile signals. No robot needed.
