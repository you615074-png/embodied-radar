HubEgo-100K: 10,000 Hours of First-Person Work Video, Now on Hugging Face

DatasetDatasetsHubLabs/HubEgo-100K

What it is: HubEgo-100K is a Hugging Face dataset of first-person (egocentric) video showing people doing skilled manual work in real workplaces — construction sites, workshops, salons, kitchens, shops, warehouses, and homes. This first release contains 10,124 hours of RGB video plus IMU data from 12,227 contributors, published in LeRobot and MCAP formats under CC-BY-4.0. The full plan is 100,000 hours, released in stages.

Why it matters: Robot learning needs lots of human demonstration data that looks like what a robot would see and do. Egocentric video is a cheap way to collect that at scale — no teleoperation, no robot hardware, just people filming their own work. If the data is clean and well-formatted, it can be used for imitation learning and pretraining vision-language-action (VLA) models. The fact that it ships in LeRobot format means it plugs into an existing open-source training stack.

Can you try it without a robot? Yes. You can load a small slice with the LeRobot library on Hugging Face and visualize the video and IMU streams. You won't train a full policy on a laptop, but you can inspect the format and run a small experiment.

Caveats: the authors/organization behind it are not clearly stated, and the dataset page lists a future release date, so treat this as an early, partial release until verified.
