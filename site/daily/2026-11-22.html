Can LLMs Debug Robots by Editing Policy Weights?

RepoVLA & foundation modelsjushengzhang/robot-weight-debugging

This is the first research release of a paper by Jusheng Zhang (Stanford), Keze Wang and Liang Lin (X-Era AI) asking whether a large language model can debug a robot policy by editing its weights directly. The setup: a pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policy, π₀.₅, fails on paraphrased instructions. GPT-6 Astra inspects the policy's weights and, using budgeted simulator feedback, edits 65 rows of the token embedding — no gradient updates, no new demonstrations.

The headline result, per the paper: on 765 held-out LIBERO-Plus paraphrases, the best of three independent editing sessions raises π₀.₅ success from 86.3% to 92.0% (48 repairs, 4 regressions). The gain is concentrated on instructions containing the edited tokens (74.3% → 87.5% on 335 episodes); the other 430 episodes show zero binary outcome changes. The repo includes code, all 16 editing-session transcripts, episode-level records, and the evaluated edit.

Why it matters: it suggests a cheap repair path for instruction-following failures that doesn't require retraining or collecting data — closer to patching software than training a model. But caveats are real: GPT-6 Astra is closed-source, so the editing process isn't fully reproducible; results are simulation-only; and the authors flag that V2 and real-robot experiments are in progress.

Can you try it without a robot? Not really. It needs LIBERO simulation and π₀.₅, which won't run on a Mac. You can read the transcripts and videos, but redoing the weight edits locally isn't realistic.
