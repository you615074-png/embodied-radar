RoboColosseum: a distributed benchmark for VLA policies on real robots

PaperVLA & foundation modelsRoboColosseum: Evaluating Physical Intelligence at Scale In-The-Wild

What it is. RoboColosseum is a distributed evaluation framework from Tan Sang Nguyen, Yi Ru Wang, Shailesh Shailesh, Gang Yang, Jiamo Hu, Jianuo Jiang, Haoquan Fang and Zhongzheng Ren. Instead of everyone testing models on the same robot in one lab, it connects locally operated robots at many sites under a shared protocol. The first study covers five vision-language-action (VLA) policies and three frontier AI agents across four embodiments: SO-101, Franka, Bimanual-YAM, and the G1 humanoid. In the Open Track, models are compared blinded and head-to-head without task-specific tuning; the Fine-tune Track gives every policy the same 100 demonstrations per task across 20 tasks.

Why it matters. Across 5,699 pairwise comparisons at 124 sites, the authors report that frontier agents come close to specialized VLA policies, that all systems still struggle on open-world tasks, and that ranking agreement across embodiment-specific task suites is weak (Kendall W = 0.24). Open Track rankings also poorly predict post-fine-tuning performance. On the G1 humanoid no policy finishes any of five tasks, yet measured partial progress ranges from 13.3% to 23.2%. In short: success rate alone hides a lot, and generalization across robots is still an open problem.

Can you try it without a robot? Not really. Participation needs a real robot connected to the network. There is no public code or weight license listed yet. You can still read the project page and results charts to understand the findings.
