ExMR: Full-Body Motion Capture from a Phone Video

RepoDatasetstwehrbein/ExMR

What it is: ExMR is the official code for a paper by Tom Wehrbein and Bodo Rosenhahn (arXiv 2610.26213, a journal extension of FootMR from 3DV 2026). It takes one ordinary monocular video and reconstructs the 3D motion of the person in it, including the body, hands, and feet, represented as SMPL-X. The pipeline first estimates whole-body motion with a temporal estimator, then two refinement modules, FootMR and HandMR, go back and look at local image evidence to predict residual joint rotations for the extremities.

Why it matters: Hands and feet are usually the worst part of monocular motion capture. ExMR is not a new foundation model; it is a careful refinement step on top of an existing estimator, and the authors are upfront that evaluation and training code are still to come. The useful part for practitioners is the output side: results are exported in a world-grounded frame, as an AMASS SMPL-X file for Blender, as BVH, as FBX via Blender for Unity and Unreal, and retargeted to humanoid robots with GMR. That makes a phone clip usable in an animation or robotics pipeline.

Can you try it without a robot? Yes. There is an online demo on Hugging Face Spaces that runs on up to 20 seconds of video in the default mode, with no installation. Local runs are tested on Linux with an NVIDIA GPU (around 8 GB VRAM), and the SAM 3D Body weights are gated on Hugging Face and approved manually, so local setup is not instant. The repo currently ships demo code only. License is marked NOASSERTION on GitHub, so check the terms before reusing anything.

Try it without a robot: Just open the Hugging Face Space demo, upload a short clip (<=20s) and see the output; no install needed.
