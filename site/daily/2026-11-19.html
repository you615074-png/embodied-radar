ForceHarness: Force Feedback for Frozen Frontier Models Without Training

PaperVLA & foundation modelsForceHarness: Turning Force into Action and Physics for Frozen Frontier Models

ForceHarness is a paper by Ke Sun, Kevin Yuchen Ma, Yike Zhang, Yanzhe Chen, and Mike Zheng Shou about giving frozen, API-only multimodal models a sense of touch. Frontier models can already control robots zero-shot from images, but many manipulation tasks depend on contact forces that cameras cannot see. The paper identifies two problems: contact dynamics evolve much faster than an API model can respond, and a frozen model cannot interpret raw force streams. ForceHarness solves the first with self-terminating force primitives that regulate force locally and return at contact events, and the second with a physics translation layer that converts each force trace into physical quantities with uncertainty as the tool result. The authors also build HiddenPhys, a seven-task MuJoCo benchmark with hidden physical variables, and report that on a real arm without a wrist force sensor, zero-shot success of GPT-6 Astra rises from 3.3% to 90.0%. In simulation it adds 17.5 points on ForceBench and 34.3 points on HiddenPhys. A key takeaway from ablations: smaller models make better decisions when given physical quantities rather than raw force values. Note: the abstract mentions GPT-6 Astra and an arXiv ID that appear beyond current reality, so verify authenticity. No license is stated; code availability is unclear. Real-arm experiments need a specific arm and force sensor, so newcomers cannot reproduce them.

Try it without a robot: Run HiddenPhys/ForceBench in MuJoCo on your Mac to reproduce sim results; real-arm experiments need hardware you don't have.
