AI2's 720-hour bimanual dataset, in LeRobot format

DatasetDatasetsallenai/MolmoAct2-BimanualYAM-Dataset

What it is. The Allen Institute for AI (AI2) released the MolmoAct2-BimanualYAM Dataset, a collection of over 720 hours of two-armed tabletop manipulation demonstrations used to train their MolmoAct2 action model. Per the dataset card it holds 32,246 episodes, about 76 million frames at 30 fps, and 34 annotated task instructions. It is stored in the LeRobot v3.0 format and licensed Apache-2.0. The robot type is listed as bi_yam_follower.

Why it matters. Open data in this size range for bimanual manipulation is still rare, and it comes already formatted for the LeRobot ecosystem, with per-episode language annotations in meta/tasks_annotated.parquet. That makes it a usable starting point for behavior-cloning experiments and for anyone learning how vision-language-action models get their training signal.

Can you try it without a robot? Yes, in a limited way. You can browse episodes — camera streams, robot state, actions, language — in AI2's LeRobot Visualizer Space on Hugging Face, or pull a few episodes with the lerobot Python library on a laptop. You cannot reproduce MolmoAct2 training from a merged copy: the card says training used a custom multi-source dataloader, and the normalization stats are weighted approximations of per-source quantiles, which the authors acknowledge can introduce bias. The card also lists no homepage or paper link yet, apart from the citation to the MolmoAct2 paper.
