NoPeWorld: A Small Head That Stops World Models From Walking Through Walls

PaperWorld models & simNon-penetrating World Models

What it is. A paper called Non-penetrating World Models by Yuhang Dai, Yinwei Wu, Ziyue Lin, Han Ji and Xingyi Yang. The authors first built NoPeBench, a benchmark that checks how interactive video world models react to physical obstacles. They found that none of the open-source models they tested could reliably stop objects from passing through walls. Their fix, NoPeWorld, trains a small prediction head on top of a frozen diffusion transformer (DiT). The head reads the model's hidden states and predicts whether a proposed action would cause penetration; unsafe actions are swapped for safe ones that keep the original intent as much as possible.

Why it matters. Interactive video world models are increasingly used as cheap simulators for robot learning. If a model happily lets an arm glide through a table, any policy trained inside it learns the wrong physics. NoPeWorld is interesting because it does not retrain the big model. It reuses information already present in the DiT's hidden states, which the authors say is enough to detect violations. Per the abstract, the average violation rate over six world models drops from 74% to 16%, and the method generalizes zero-shot to open-domain images, third-person views and moving obstacles.

Can you try it without a robot? Partly. The paper says code, the NoPeBench benchmark and model weights are released, and there is a project page with demos. But interactive video generation of this size is not realistic on a Mac with 16GB of unified memory and no NVIDIA GPU. Reading the code and the benchmark is the practical path for now.
